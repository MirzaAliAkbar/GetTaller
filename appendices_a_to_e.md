## A.2 Full Exercise Details — spine_1 (Jefferson Curl)

Target Muscles: Spine Erectors, Core, Hamstrings, Glutes, Shoulders
Equipment: None (mat optional)

STEPS:
1. Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, arms relaxed at sides
2. Lower chin toward chest, begin curling neck vertebrae one at a time
3. Continue curling through upper back, middle back, lower back
4. Let arms hang loose, curl toward floor at your own pace
5. At maximum flexion, pause 2 seconds, feel spinal stretch
6. Reverse: start from lower back, stack vertebrae one by one
7. Return to upright with engaged abs
8. Repeat for specified repetitions

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Rushing the movement (reduces articulatory benefit)
- Bending from hips instead of curling spine
- Not engaging core throughout
- Holding breath — maintain rhythmic breathing
- Using momentum to return — use control

BREATHING PATTERN:
Inhale → begin curl → exhale → curl forward → inhale at bottom
Exhale → roll up → inhale at upright

MODIFICATIONS:
BEGINNER: Seated version on chair to reduce lower back load
INTERMEDIATE: Hold light stick behind back to track alignment
ADVANCED: Add 2-5kg plate on chest at bottom position

SAFETY:
- Stop if sharp lower back pain
- Do not force beyond comfortable range
- Warm up 5 min before starting
- Consult doctor if spinal conditions exist

## A.3 Full Exercise Details — spine_2 (Cat-Cow Stretch)

Target Muscles: Spine, Core, Shoulders, Neck
Equipment: Mat (optional)

STEPS:
1. Start on hands and knees, tabletop position
2. Wrists under shoulders, knees under hips
3. Cow: inhale, drop belly, lift chin and chest, gaze up
4. Cat: exhale, round spine toward ceiling, tuck chin to chest
5. Move slowly between each position, syncing with breath
6. Focus on sequential articulation of each vertebra
7. Repeat for full duration

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Rushing through movement instead of controlled articulation
- Hyperextending neck in Cow pose
- Not engaging core throughout
- Holding breath — match breath to movement

BENEFITS:
- Improves spinal flexibility and mobility
- Relieves lower back and mid-back tension
- Enhances coordination between breath and movement
- Strengthens core and shoulder stabilizers

## A.4 Full Exercise Details — spine_3 (Cobra Stretch)

Target Muscles: Spinal Erectors, Lower Back, Abdominals (stretch)
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Lie face down on mat, palms under shoulders
2. Elbows close to body, legs extended behind
3. Inhale, press through palms, lift chest off mat
4. Keep hips pressed into mat, slight backbend
5. Hold at top for 2 seconds, looking slightly forward
6. Exhale, slowly lower back to start
7. Keep shoulders away from ears throughout

PROGRESSIONS:
BEGINNER: Only lift chest to elbows (low cobra)
INTERMEDIATE: Full cobra with straight arms
ADVANCED: Cobra with legs lifting (locust variation)

## A.5 Full Exercise Details — spine_4 (Pelvic Tilt)

Target Muscles: Lower Back, Glutes, Core, Hips
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat on floor
2. Arms at sides, palms down, neutral spine
3. Inhale, slight arch in lower back
4. Exhale, tilt pelvis upward, press lower back into mat
5. Engage glutes and core at top of tilt
6. Hold 2-3 seconds, release, repeat
7. Movement is small — focus on pelvic rotation

KEY POINTS:
- Only the pelvis moves — upper body stays still
- Movement should be about 10-15 degrees of tilt
- Breathe deeply — do not hold breath

## A.6 Full Exercise Details — spine_5 (Cat-Camel Stretch)

Target Muscles: Full Spine, Core, Shoulders
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Same start as Cat-Cow (tabletop position)
2. Emphasis on slower, more deliberate spinal articulation
3. Camel: round spine up, imagine holding coin between shoulder blades
4. Cat: reverse, drop belly, full spinal extension
5. Each phase takes 5 seconds — maximum mindfulness
6. Focus on sensation in each spinal segment

DIFFERENCE FROM CAT-COW:
Cat-Camel is SLOWER (5s per direction vs 3s), MORE DELIBERATE, and often used as a warm-up before heavier spine work.

## A.7 Full Exercise Details — hanging_1 (Dead Hang)

Target Muscles: Grip, Upper Back, Shoulders, Spine (decompression)
Equipment: Pull-up bar

STEPS:
1. Jump or step up to grasp pull-up bar, overhand grip
2. Hands shoulder-width apart, full passive hang
3. Shoulders relaxed up toward ears (passive), not engaged
4. Feet off ground, body hanging straight
5. Feel spine lengthening through gravity
6. Hold for specified duration, breathing deeply
7. Release carefully, do not jump down

KEY POINTS:
- FULLY relax shoulders — this is a decompression exercise
- Keep grip active but body relaxed
- Feel the stretch through entire spine
- Breathe deeply — decompression increases with full breaths

PROGRESSIONS:
BEGINNER: Assisted (toes touching ground, partial weight)
INTERMEDIATE: Full dead hang, 20-30 seconds
ADVANCED: One-arm assisted or weighted vest

SAFETY:
- Ensure bar is stable and can support body weight
- Use crash mat or padding under bar
- Do not use if shoulder injury exists
- Release grip gradually — sudden drop can injure shoulders

## A.8 Full Exercise Details — hanging_2 (Active Hang)

Target Muscles: Lats, Core, Shoulders, Grip, Upper Back
Equipment: Pull-up bar

STEPS:
1. Same grip as dead hang, overhand, shoulder-width
2. Actively pull shoulders DOWN and BACK (scapular depression)
3. Engage lats, brace core, slight posterior pelvic tilt
4. Body forms slight hollow shape (not arched)
5. Hold active position, maintaining all muscle engagement
6. Keep breathing — do not hold breath under tension

DIFFERENCE FROM DEAD HANG:
Dead hang = passive (relaxed shoulders, gravity stretches)
Active hang = engaged (shoulders down, lats on, core braced)

## A.9 Full Exercise Details — hanging_3 (Scapular Pull-ups)

Target Muscles: Scapular Retractors, Lats, Rhomboids, Lower Traps
Equipment: Pull-up bar

STEPS:
1. Hang from bar with active grip (shoulders engaged down)
2. Without bending elbows, pull shoulder blades DOWN and TOGETHER
3. Body rises 2-4 inches from scapular retraction alone
4. Lower back to start with control
5. Focus on mind-muscle connection with scapular muscles
6. Repeat for specified reps

KEY POINTS:
- DO NOT bend elbows — all movement comes from shoulder blades
- Imagine squeezing a pencil between shoulder blades
- This is the foundation movement for pull-ups

## A.12 Full Exercise Details — hip_3 (Figure 4 Stretch)

Target Muscles: Piriformis, Glutes, External Rotators
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat on floor
2. Cross right ankle over left knee (figure 4 shape)
3. Reach through, grasp left thigh, pull toward chest
4. Keep right foot flexed to protect knee
5. Feel stretch in right glute and piriformis
6. Hold 30 seconds, switch sides

KEY POINTS:
- Foot MUST be flexed (dorsiflexed) to protect knee
- Pull gently — do not force knee toward chest
- Great for sciatica relief and hip mobility

## A.13 Full Exercise Details — leg_1 (PNF Hamstring Stretch)

Target Muscles: Hamstrings, Calves, Lower Back
Equipment: Mat, Stretch Band (optional)

PNF METHOD (Contract-Relax):
1. Lie on back, one leg extended, other leg raised 90 degrees
2. Hold raised leg with band or hands behind thigh
3. Phase 1 (6s): Push raised leg DOWN against resistance (isometric)
4. Phase 2 (10s): Relax, gently pull leg CLOSER toward head
5. Phase 3 (6s): Push again (deeper range this time)
6. Repeat 3-4 cycles per leg

WHY PNF?
PNF (Proprioceptive Neuromuscular Facilitation) uses the inverse myotatic reflex. During the isometric contraction, Golgi tendon organs signal the muscle to relax, allowing a DEEPER stretch after release. This is 30-50% more effective than static stretching.

## A.14 Full Exercise Details — leg_2 (Calf Raises)

Target Muscles: Gastrocnemius, Soleus, Ankle Stabilizers
Equipment: Step, Stair, or Edge

STEPS:
1. Stand on edge of step, heels hanging off
2. Lower heels below step level (deep calf stretch)
3. Press through balls of feet, raise heels HIGH as possible
4. Hold top position 2 seconds, squeeze calves
5. Lower back to start (below step level)
6. Controlled tempo: 2s down, 1s hold, 2s up

VARIATIONS:
STRAIGHT LEG: Targets gastrocnemius (upper calf)
BENT KNEE: Targets soleus (lower calf)
SINGLE LEG: Double intensity, requires balance

BENEFITS FOR HEIGHT:
Calf raises improve blood circulation in legs, stimulate bone density, and develop lower body support for spinal decompression exercises.

## A.15 Full Exercise Details — core_1 (Vacuum Exercise)

Target Muscles: Transverse Abdominis (deep core), Diaphragm
Equipment: None

STEPS:
1. Stand or kneel — exhale all air from lungs
2. Pull belly button IN and UP toward spine as far as possible
3. Hold the vacuum position, do NOT breathe (or tiny breaths)
4. Imagine bringing navel to spine
5. Hold for specified duration
6. Release slowly, inhale, rest 15 seconds
7. Progress to longer holds over time

BENEFITS:
- Strengthens deep core (Transverse Abdominis)
- Improves posture through better core engagement
- Creates corset effect for waistline
- Essential for spinal stability during hanging
- Helps prevent lower back pain

PROGRESSIONS:
BEGINNER: 10s holds x 3, kneeling position
INTERMEDIATE: 20s holds x 3, standing
ADVANCED: 30s+ holds x 3, during active movement

## A.16 Full Exercise Details — core_2 (Dead Bug)

Target Muscles: Deep Core, Rectus Abdominis, Hip Flexors
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Lie on back, arms extended toward ceiling, legs in tabletop
2. Knees at 90 degrees, shins parallel to floor
3. Press entire lower back into mat — no arch
4. Simultaneously extend RIGHT arm overhead + LEFT leg straight out
5. Keep hovering 2 inches off ground, do not touch floor
6. Return to center, repeat with LEFT arm + RIGHT leg
7. Move slowly with control — do not rush
8. Continue alternating for duration

KEY POINTS:
- Lower back must stay pressed into mat throughout
- If lower back arches -> reduce range of motion
- Breathe naturally — do not hold breath
- Quality over speed — slow and controlled

BENEFITS:
- Excellent core stability builder
- Improves anti-rotation control
- Develops limb coordination with core engagement

## A.17 Full Exercise Details — core_3 (Bird Dog)

Target Muscles: Core, Spinal Stabilizers, Glutes, Shoulders
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Start on hands and knees, tabletop position
2. Brace core, maintain flat back (like table)
3. Extend RIGHT arm forward + LEFT leg back simultaneously
4. Keep arm and leg parallel to floor, do not lift above body
5. Do NOT rotate hips or shoulders — stay square
6. Hold extended position 2 seconds
7. Return to start with control
8. Alternate sides

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Lifting arm/leg too high (above parallel)
- Rotating hips when lifting leg
- Dropping belly toward floor (arching)
- Rushing through reps

## A.18 Full Exercise Details — posture_1 (Wall Angels)

Target Muscles: Thoracic Extension, Shoulder External Rotation, Upper Back
Equipment: Wall

STEPS:
1. Stand with back against wall, heels 6 inches from wall
2. Press head, upper back, and tailbone against wall
3. Arms at 90 degrees, elbows and wrists touching wall
4. Slowly slide arms UP overhead keeping contact with wall
5. Squeeze shoulder blades together throughout
6. Go as high as possible without losing wall contact
7. Lower arms back to start position

KEY POINTS:
- All 3 contact points (head, upper back, tailbone) must stay on wall
- If arms can't go full range without arching -> go less range
- Ribs should not flare out — keep core braced

BENEFITS:
- Directly improves rounded shoulder posture
- Opens tight chest muscles
- Strengthens scapular retractors

Target Muscles: Spinal Erectors, Glutes, Hamstrings, Rear Delts
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Lie face down on mat, arms extended overhead
2. Simultaneously lift arms, chest, and legs off mat
3. Keep neck neutral (gaze 6 inches ahead of hands)
4. Squeeze glutes and lower back muscles
5. Hold position, breathing throughout
6. Lower with control to start

KEY POINTS:
- DO NOT jerk into position — lift smoothly
- Legs should be straight but not locked
- Arms parallel to floor, palms facing down

PROGRESSIONS:
ALTERNATING: Lift opposite arm + leg
WEIGHTED: Hold 1-2kg weights in hands
HOLD: Up to 60 seconds

## A.21 Full Exercise Details — neck_1 (Chin Tucks)

Target Muscles: Deep Cervical Flexors, Upper Traps (stretch)
Equipment: None

STEPS:
1. Sit or stand with tall spine, shoulders relaxed
2. Level head (imagine string pulling from top of head)
3. Without tilting head down, pull chin STRAIGHT BACK
4. You should feel a stretch at base of skull and upper neck
5. You will develop a double chin appearance — this is correct
6. Hold for 5-10 seconds, release, repeat
7. Do NOT tilt head up or down — purely horizontal glide

KEY POINTS:
- This is a subtle movement — only 1-2 cm of range
- Think head retraction not chin down
- Keep jaw relaxed — teeth should not clench

BENEFITS:
- Corrects forward head posture (text neck)
- Relieves tension headaches from poor posture
- Strengthens deep neck flexors

## A.22 Full Exercise Details — neck_2 (Neck Isometric Exercises)

Target Muscles: All Neck Musculature (Flexors, Extensors, Lateral Flexors)
Equipment: None (use hand resistance)

DIRECTIONS:
- Perform each direction for 10s x 3
- 50% effort — do NOT max out
- Breathe throughout — do not hold breath
- Keep jaw relaxed, shoulders down

MOVEMENTS:
1. Forward: Hand on forehead, push head forward, resist with neck
2. Backward: Hands behind head, push head back, resist with neck
3. Left: Hand on left temple, push head left, resist
4. Right: Hand on right temple, push head right, resist

SAFETY:
- Do NOT hold breath — increases blood pressure unnecessarily
- Do NOT rotate or tilt head — pure resistance in each plane
- Stop if sharp pain or dizziness

## A.23 Full Exercise Details — breathing_1 (Diaphragmatic Breathing)

Target: Diaphragm, Parasympathetic Nervous System, Stress Reduction
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat, one hand on chest, one on belly
2. Inhale slowly through nose (4 seconds) — belly rises, chest stays still
3. Pause at top of inhale (2 seconds)
4. Exhale slowly through mouth (6 seconds) — belly falls
5. Hand on belly should move more than hand on chest
6. Repeat for duration, focus on slow, deep, even breath

WHY IT MATTERS FOR HEIGHT:
Diaphragmatic breathing increases intra-abdominal pressure, which stabilizes the spine and core. Deep breathing also signals the body to relax, lowering cortisol. High cortisol inhibits growth hormone production. Deep, slow breathing maximizes HGH secretion during rest.

PROGRESSION:
WEEK 1: 3 min x 3 daily, lying down
WEEK 2: 5 min x 3 daily, add seated position
WEEK 3+: 5 min x 3 daily, standing, during daily activities

## A.25 Full Exercise Details — yoga_1 (Downward Dog)

Target: Full Posterior Chain, Shoulders, Core, Hamstrings, Calves
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Start on hands and knees, tabletop position
2. Tuck toes under, lift knees off mat, push hips UP and BACK
3. Arms straight, head between arms, ears between biceps
4. Press palms firmly into mat, spread fingers wide
5. Push chest toward thighs, heels reaching toward floor
6. Pedal heels alternately to stretch calves
7. Hold for 30 seconds, breathing deeply
8. To release: lower knees to mat, return to tabletop

KEY POINTS:
- Shape is an inverted V — not a pike
- Keep spine long — do not round upper back
- Bend knees slightly if hamstrings are tight

BENEFITS:
- Full body stretch — elongates entire posterior chain
- Strengthens shoulders, arms, and legs
- Promotes spinal lengthening and decompression

## A.27 Full Exercise Details — yoga_3 (Mountain Pose — Tadasana)

Target: Posture, Body Awareness, Alignment, Core
Equipment: None

STEPS:
1. Stand with feet together (or hip-width), big toes touching
2. Distribute weight evenly across both feet
3. Engage quadriceps (pull knee caps up)
4. Tuck tailbone slightly (neutral pelvis)
5. Draw belly in and up (engage core)
6. Roll shoulders back and down, arms at sides
7. Crown of head reaches toward ceiling
8. Gaze forward softly, breathe naturally
9. Hold 30-60 seconds

KEY POINTS:
- This is NOT just standing — it's active alignment
- Imagine a string pulling from crown of head to ceiling
- Feel the length through entire spine

BENEFITS:
- Establishes proper standing alignment
- Corrects postural imbalances
- Foundation for all standing yoga poses

## A.29 Full Exercise Details — stretch_4 (Standing Forward Fold)

Target: Hamstrings, Calves, Lower Back, Spine (traction)
Equipment: None

STEPS:
1. Stand with feet hip-width apart, knees slightly bent
2. Exhale, hinge at hips, fold forward from hips (not waist)
3. Let head and arms hang heavy toward floor
4. Hold opposite elbows, sway gently side to side
5. Shift weight slightly forward to toes
6. Feel traction through entire spine
7. To come up: bend knees deeply, stack vertebrae one at a time

KEY POINTS:
- Bend knees as needed — especially if hamstrings are tight
- All forward fold comes from hip hinge, not spinal rounding
- This is a decompression posture for the spine

## A.32 Full Exercise Details — advanced_1 (Full Bridge)

Target: Full Body — Spine, Glutes, Core, Shoulders, Hips, Legs
Equipment: Mat

STEPS:
1. Lie on back, knees bent, feet flat, hands by ears (fingers toward feet)
2. Press through hands and feet, lift hips, back, shoulders off mat
3. Straighten arms as flexibility allows (do not force)
4. Head hangs between shoulders, gaze between hands
5. Press hips up — do not let knees splay
6. Hold 10-20 seconds, breathing
7. To release: tuck chin, lower upper back first, then hips

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Letting knees fall outward -> creates knee strain
- Holding breath -> increases blood pressure
- Pushing through head instead of hands -> neck strain

PROGRESSIONS:
BEGINNER: Glute bridge (shoulders on ground)
INTERMEDIATE: Half bridge (partial elbow extension)
ADVANCED: Full bridge (straight arms)
EXPERT: Walking bridge or one-leg bridge

SAFETY:
- Do NOT attempt if wrist, shoulder, or back injury
- Warm up wrists thoroughly (circles, flex/extend)
- Stop if sharp wrist or lower back pain

## A.33 Full Exercise Details — advanced_2 (L-Sit Hold)

Target: Full Core, Hip Flexors, Triceps, Shoulders, Quads
Equipment: Parallel bars, sturdy chairs, or floor

STEPS:
1. Sit between parallel bars or on floor with hands by hips (fingers forward)
2. Press through palms, lift entire body off surface
3. Extend legs straight in front, parallel to ground
4. Keep legs together, point toes, engage entire core
5. Press shoulders down (depress scapula)
6. Hold as long as possible with good form

KEY POINTS:
- Shoulders must be DEPRESSED (away from ears)
- If legs can't extend fully -> keep tucked (tuck L-sit)
- Squeeze inner thighs together for leg stability

PROGRESSIONS:
TUCK L-SIT: Knees tucked to chest (easiest)
ADVANCED TUCK: Shins parallel to ground
ONE-LEG L: One leg extended, one tucked
FULL L-SIT: Both legs fully extended
V-SIT: Legs above parallel (advanced)

## A.34 Full Exercise Details — balance_1 (Single Leg Stand)

Target: Balance, Proprioception, Ankle, Core, Glutes
Equipment: None (wall optional for support)

STEPS:
1. Stand tall, shift weight onto left foot
2. Lift right foot off ground, bring sole to left calf or thigh
3. Fix gaze on a single spot (Drishti)

6. Switch sides and repeat

PROGRESSIONS:
BEGINNER: Near wall, finger for balance
INTERMEDIATE: Arms at sides, no support
ADVANCED: Eyes closed, or on unstable surface

BENEFITS:
- Improves body awareness and control
- Strengthens ankle stabilizers
- Corrects hip imbalances (left vs right)

## A.35 Full Exercise Details — balance_2 (Tree Pose — Vrksasana)

Target: Balance, Hip Abductors, Core, Standing Stability
Equipment: None

STEPS:
1. Start in mountain pose (Tadasana), hands on hips
2. Shift weight to left foot, root down through all 4 corners
3. Place right foot: to calf (beginner) or inner thigh (advanced)
4. DO NOT place foot on knee joint — always above or below
5. Hips face forward — do not rotate open
6. When balanced, bring hands to prayer at chest or overhead
7. Fix gaze on steady point, hold 30 seconds
8. Release, switch sides

KEY POINTS:
- Press foot into thigh AND thigh into foot (mutual pressure)
- Standing leg is straight but not locked
- Breath is steady — do not hold breath

## A.36 Full Exercise Details — fascia_1 (Foam Roller Back)

Target: Thoracic Fascia, Spinal Erectors, Latissimus Dorsi, Upper Back
Equipment: Foam Roller

STEPS:
1. Lie on back, roller placed under shoulder blades
2. Hands behind head or crossed on chest
3. Lift hips slightly, use legs to roll back and forth
4. Roll from mid-back to just below shoulders
5. DO NOT roll on lower back or neck
6. Pause on tight spots for 30 seconds or until release
7. Roll slowly — 1 inch per second
8. Continue for duration

KEY POINTS:
- Only roll MUSCLE — never on bone or joint
- DO NOT roll lower back (lumbar) — risk of injury
- DO NOT roll neck (cervical) — arteries there

BENEFITS:
- Releases tight upper back fascia from poor posture
- Improves thoracic spine mobility
- Reduces muscle tension before exercises

SAFETY:
- NEVER roll directly on spine bones
- Avoid if bruising easily or on blood thinners
- Do not roll more than 2 minutes on one area

## A.37 Full Exercise Details — shoulder_1 (Shoulder Dislocations)

Target: Shoulder Mobility, Thoracic Extension, Lats, Pecs
Equipment: Stretch band, towel, or PVC pipe

NOTE: The name is misleading — the shoulder does NOT dislocate. This is a dislocate stretch for shoulder mobility.

STEPS:
1. Hold band/towel with wide grip (much wider than shoulders)
2. Begin in front of hips, arms straight
3. Slowly raise arms overhead and behind body
4. Continue until band touches lower back OR range limit
5. Reverse movement to bring back to start
6. Narrow grip as mobility improves

KEY POINTS:
- Start with VERY WIDE grip — narrow only as mobility allows
- Keep arms straight throughout (do NOT bend elbows)
- Move slowly — this is advanced shoulder mobility

PROGRESSIONS:
WIDE GRIP: ~5+ feet apart (easiest mobility)
MEDIUM GRIP: ~3-4 feet apart (intermediate)
NARROW GRIP: ~2 feet apart (advanced)
TOUCH: Band touches lower back (expert)

SAFETY:
- Do NOT attempt if shoulder injury, impingement, or instability
- Warm up shoulders thoroughly first (arm circles, wall slides)
- Stop immediately if sharp pain or popping
